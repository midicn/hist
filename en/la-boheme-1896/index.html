La bohème Premieres

Puccini's La bohème put ordinary poverty and conversation on the operatic stage in Turin, 1896.

1 min readMilestonesUpdated 2026-10-28

PucciniItalian operaTurin1896

Related

RomanticOperaVerismoVoice

The year it happened

1 February 1896, Turin, at the Teatro Regio. Puccini's four-act La bohème was conducted by Toscanini, with Ferrani as Mimì and Gorga as Rodolfo. The libretto by Illica and Giacosa was based on Murger's Scènes de la vie de bohème.

What took place

The opera brings a Latin Quarter garret onto the stage, with a poor poet, a painter, a musician and a seamstress. Act I is not a spectacle but lighting the stove, hunting for a key, touching hands and singing Che gelida manina. Act II is Christmas Eve outside the Café Momus; Act III is a city barrier in the snow, and Act IV returns to the garret, where Mimì is dying and friends cover farewell with jokes. Puccini lets the orchestra follow the pauses and breath of speech; melody grows out of conversation, and chorus and solo pass quietly between each other.

What was different after

Opera could be about ordinary people, the poor and the sick, without kings, priests or war. Everyday talk, chamber intimacy and sudden lyricism could share one room, and the verismo turn found a new stage in city life. The first night was cool and reviews split, but the work soon spread through Italy and Europe and became one of the most performed operas.

Hear the turn

Begin with the dialogue of Act I, the orchestra leaving gaps as friends grope in the dark. Then Che gelida manina, where the line rises from speech into song. Finally a passage from Tosca, where the same composer presses harder and tightens the drama. Two Puccini pressures.
