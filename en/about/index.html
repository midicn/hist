About Music History

How this music history is organised, what it will contain, and how to read it.

What this station is for

Music history is the timeline of the whole library. It does not treat a work or an instrument in isolation; it answers what was happening in music at a given moment, and how that relates to what came before and after.

What the timeline will hold

  • Period overviews — from early monophonic chant to the present; each period gets a piece explaining what its music was doing, why, and what it left behind.
  • Milestones — concrete events: a premiere, a treatise, a change to an instrument, the arrival of a new kind of venue. Each entry explains only what it changed.
  • Features — threads that cross periods, such as the shift in notation, the growth of the orchestra, and the move of performance from church to concert hall.

Chinese music appears here only as a short comparison — what was happening in China at the same moment. The depth lives on the Chinese music station, so nothing is written twice.

What each entry looks like

  • It opens with time and place, then says what happened and why it mattered.
  • Key entries offer tracks you can play straight from the library, so you can hear the writing of the time.
  • Written in both Chinese and English, with the same dates and facts in each.

How to read it

1. In order, by period — walk it end to end and watch the threads connect. 2. Across, by feature — follow notation, instruments or venues across periods. 3. At the Chinese comparison — go straight from there to the Chinese music station.

How it will be released

Periods and milestones are being added over time. This station only makes sense once the timeline holds together, so it is being built one period at a time rather than as a scattering of early entries.
