Shanghai Huju

Huju turned Shanghai tanhuang from sitting storytelling into city opera, with long-chang tunes and the Zizhu melody

1 min readChinese MusicUpdated 2026-10-30

hujutanhuanglong-changShanghai

Time and place

Huju grew in Shanghai between the late Qing and the early twentieth century. Field songs and flower-drum storytelling on both banks of the Huangpu combined into tanhuang, sung sitting down in teahouses and on the street as bentan. Early in the century it moved into amusement halls as shenqu, and from the 1940s it was generally called huju.

What happened

Tanhuang began as a handful of singers sharing roles around a table. In the city the performers learned from spoken drama, took up acts, sets and stage lighting, and wrote plays in Qing dress and in Western suits, playing contemporary life in modern clothes. The repertory runs from Lu Yachen and Meeting in the Nunnery to Burying Love in the Yellow Spring, Luohan Qian and Reed Marshes. The singing shifted from strings of qupai to long-chang tunes as the backbone, with small tunes kept as interludes.

Musical features

The vocal system is mainly banqiang. Long-chang is the frame, starting unhurried with lines stretched wide; sanjiao ban and fuzi ban drive quick exchanges; yangxue and yinyangxue carry grief. Qupai such as Zizhu diao, Wujiang ge, xiuxiang and mihun diao survive. The shenhu, a lead erhu, is joined by pipa, yangqin and flute, with no gongs and no martial band. Singing uses Shanghainese in a natural voice, phrases often sliding downward.

What to listen for

Begin with Shanghai Zizhu diao, a Jiangnan shidiao used by huju as a tuneful interlude; hear the ornaments and the light duple beat. Then Wujiang ge, a local tune with a weeping slide. Gugongchen tells a local story, so you can hear Shanghainese tones settling onto melody. All three are folk songs, not huju arias.

Common misunderstandings

Huju is often imagined as old, or as Shanghai's answer to Yueju. It is barely a century old, converted from tanhuang storytelling into opera, with no martial plays and no gong-and-drum band. Yueju comes from Zhejiang, with different speech and different tunes.
