Ignaz Friedman

One of the finest mazurka writers after Chopin and a last Romantic pianist

Related

RomanticPianoMazurka

Who he was

Ignaz Friedman (1882–1948) was born at Podgórze, just outside Kraków, and became a Polish pianist and composer. He studied musicology with Riemann in Leipzig, then piano with Leschetizky in Vienna from 1904, and afterwards toured Europe, the Americas and Australia as a soloist. He moved to Sydney in 1940 and died there in 1948. He left about ninety works, nearly all connected with the piano.

His place

He belongs to the last generation of Romantic pianist-composers, for whom playing and writing were one trade, with Chopin, Schumann and Liszt at the centre of the repertoire. He took no part in modernism and his harmony stays within late Romantic bounds, yet his mazurkas are excellent and count among the most important continuations of the genre after Chopin. His playing was known for free tempo and thick sonority, and those habits are written into his music. His playing survives only on record, where the free tempo is still studied as a lesson.

What to hear

Listen first for the mazurka accent. The stress often falls on the second or third beat, so the step seems to miss its footing; Op.85 makes that irregularity plain. Then the bass as pivot: left-hand chords often arrive early or late rather than on the beat, which gives the phrase breath. Then patience in variation — the Passacaglia builds a whole piece on one repeated bass, adding a layer at each variation. Finally the decisiveness of the preludes, whose endings often stop rather than fade.

Key works

  • Piano: Six Mazurkas Op.85, Passacaglia Op.44, Eight Preludes Op.80
  • Studies: Studies on a Theme of Paganini, Three Concert Studies
  • Other: three volumes of Polnische Lyrik, Wiener Tänze

Influence

Friedman is remembered today mainly as a performer, and his recordings of Chopin and Mendelssohn remain evidence of Romantic performing practice. His composing is the echo of that tradition — not new, but written to the end of a genre, so that later Polish composers approaching a mazurka still have to get past him. Few of his works were published, and he introduced most of them himself in recital. He concertised in South America and Australia before settling in Sydney.
