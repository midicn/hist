The Formation of Yue Opera

Yue Opera began as tale-singing in Shengzhou, moved to Shanghai, and was reshaped by women's troupes and new tune systems.

Time and place

In spring 1906, tale-singers Li Shiquan and Gao Binghuo put on makeup and performed on a makeshift stage in Dongwang village, Shengzhou, Zhejiang. Their shows were called xiaoge ban; they reached Shanghai around 1917 and were known in the 1920s as Shaoxing wenxi, taking the name Yue Opera in the 1940s.

Vocal style and musical features

Yue Opera is sung in Shengzhou dialect and uses banqiang form. The early sigong mode tuned la-mi and moved briskly; in 1943 Yuan Xuefen and colleagues created the chidiao, tuned sol-re and suited to lyric expression; in 1945 the xiandiao added a low, mournful register tuned do-sol. Ban types include manban, zhongban, kuaiban, qingban and xiaoban; qingban drops the strings and keeps only a drum click. The yuehu leads, with pipa, yangqin, flute and xiao.

Representative plays

The Butterfly Lovers, Dream of the Red Chamber, Romance of the Western Chamber, Sister Xianglin, The Carp Fairy and The Jade Hairpin, mostly scholar-beauty dramas with some modern subjects.

What to listen for

Start with the Jiangsu narrative song Shanbo Thinks of Yingtai, which shares the Butterfly Lovers story and lets you hear Wu phrase rhythm; then the Shanghai-collected Meng Jiangnu and Zizhu Diao for the turns of Jiangnan tune. In Yue Opera, follow the rise and fall of chidiao and the silence of qingban.

Common misunderstandings

Yue Opera is often confused with Shao Opera, or assumed to have always been all-female. It began in Shengzhou, was once called Shaoxing wenxi, and differs from Shao Opera; early troupes were male, and only after the 1923 women's classes did the all-female style dominate.
