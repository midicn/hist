The Long-Playing Record

New York in 1948, where one record could hold twenty-five minutes of music.

1 min read25 characters条目MilestonesUpdated 2026-10-28

LPvinylrecordingalbum

Related

ContemporaryTimbrePiano

On the timeline

New York, June 1948. Columbia Records released the 33⅓ rpm long-playing record. Twelve inches across, it used finer grooves to extend each side from a few minutes to more than twenty. In 1949 RCA introduced the 45 rpm single, and the two formats competed in the market.

What took place

A 78 rpm side held only three to five minutes, so a symphony was cut across many discs and listeners kept changing records. The LP used vinyl and a slower speed to solve capacity, letting one disc hold a whole concerto or half a symphony. Labels packed several LPs into boxes with covers and notes, and classical music benefited first. Engineers began to design running order for an album.

What was different after

The unit of listening moved from the single to the album. Composers and performers could plan a twenty-minute side, pop music gained the album concept, and classical music gained complete recorded versions. Cover design, record criticism and hi-fi equipment grew around it. The LP also made recording a text to compare, setting the album frame that CD and streaming later inherited.

Hear the turn

Mozart's Requiem runs about forty-three minutes, enough to feel the edge of a disc. The chorus and orchestra of the German Requiem need continuous breath, and the LP reduced the breaks caused by turning records. In the Tristan excerpt listen to harmony that refuses to resolve, then imagine the whole opera in a box of records.
