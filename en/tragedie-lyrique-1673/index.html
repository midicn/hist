French Opera Codified

Paris in 1673, when Lully gave French opera a privilege, a theatre and a template

1 min read22 characters条目MilestonesUpdated 2026-10-30

music historyFrench operaLullyBaroque

Related

French opera was not copied from Italy. In Paris in 1673, Lully used a royal privilege, a theatre and a set of conventions to turn sung French tragedy into a repeatable genre.

The year, the place

Paris, 1673. Lully obtained the opera privilege for the Academie Royale de Musique in 1672, and Cadmus et Hermione followed in 1673 as the first tragedie en musique. He produced roughly one a year, with Armide in 1686 as the summit. Performances moved between court and city theatre.

What took place

Lully did not import Italian opera. He wrote recitative that follows French declamation, using dotted rhythm, rests and bass motion to imitate the weight of speech. Each act contains divertissements, with chorus and ballet serving court taste. The opening uses a slow-then-fast overture, later called the French overture. Five acts, mythological plots, stage machinery and a prologue praising the king made a template others could copy. The genre did not appear from nothing, since court ballet and comedie-ballet had already put dance, chorus and drama on one stage. Rameau later inherited the template, and the five-act shape with prologue lasted into the mid-eighteenth century.

What it rewrote

France acquired an opera institution, with privilege, theatre, orchestra, publishing and royal patronage tied together. Recitative became the basis of French stage music; Rameau continued it, and Gluck still measured his reforms against it. The rhythm of the French overture travelled to Germany, where Bach used it to open suites and sacred works. Opera became a trade with a fixed house, a season and an audience, not only a court ceremony.

Listen for the turn

Begin with Bois epais from Armide, hearing the orchestra hold French stress with dotted figures. Then hear the Turkish ceremony in Le bourgeois gentilhomme, the same courtly idiom used for comedy. Finally the dances of Ballet des saisons show dance carrying structure rather than decorating it. The catalogue offers selections and arrangements; listen to phrase and rhythm, not original scoring.
