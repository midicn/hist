Romantic

1820–1900: individuality, piano literature and the art song pushed music toward personal expression.

1 min read34 characters核心PeriodsUpdated 2026-10-28

Between 1820 and 1900 European music moved from writing a form well to saying a self. It runs from a piano whisper to an opera-house roar; expression comes before rule.

The historical setting

After the Napoleonic wars, the Congress of Vienna rebuilt Europe, and railways and cities carried concerts into public halls. A larger middle class bought pianos and scores; piano making and publishing became industries. The revolutions of 1848 and nationalism made the nation a subject. Musicians no longer depended only on court or church posts: solo stars, newspapers and agents manufactured public fame.

What you hear

Melodies grow longer and more vocal, often pushing against the beat through rubato and irregular accents. Harmony stretches tension with ninths, augmented sixths and distant modulations; Wagner pushes chromaticism to the edge of key. Dynamics, register and pedal resonance become expressive. Programme music writes literature, painting and autobiography into structure; Berlioz threads a fixed idea through five movements.

Genres and forms

The Lied fuses poem and piano; Schubert's Erlkönig switches one voice among four characters. Solo piano produces the ballade, nocturne, étude, impromptu, song without words and character piece, from ternary to free variation. Symphonies grow longer, concertos more virtuosic; the symphonic poem and overture appear. Opera divides into grand opera, lyric opera and Wagner's music drama, woven from leitmotifs into continuous music.

Instruments and forces

The modern piano settles in the 1800s: cast-iron plate, overstringing, double escapement and sustain pedal increase volume and resonance, making the piano the centre of home and hall. The orchestra adds valved brass, bass tuba, English horn and more percussion, while woodwind and strings expand towards quadruple winds. The saxophone enters bands and opera in the 1840s. Instrument making and technique drive each other.

Representative composers

  • Franz Schubert (1797–1828) turned the German Lied into complete dramatic scenes, his harmony pivoting from major to minor inside a line.
  • Frédéric Chopin (1810–1849) wrote almost only for piano, yet made the mazurka, nocturne and ballade carry national memory and private speech.
  • Richard Wagner (1813–1883) rebuilt opera as music drama, binding text, stage and orchestra through leitmotifs and chromatic harmony.
  • Johannes Brahms (1833–1897) filled classical forms with dense motivic work and asymmetric phrasing, proving the old shapes could still grow.

What to listen for

Listen first to how the melody speaks: phrases are stretched or cut short, accents fall off the beat. Then follow the bass: a chromatic descending line pushes harmony far from home. Finally listen to colour: piano pedal, string vibrato and valved brass thicken the same line. The five examples sample song, piano ballade, opera, symphony and requiem, showing how one period writes for different rooms.

China in the same years

In China the same decades span the late Qing and the Self-Strengthening Movement. Peking opera takes shape in Beijing, while gongche notation, kunqu and silk-and-bamboo music continue. Western brass bands appear in treaty ports, and school songs emerge as numbered notation and staff notation enter new schools. Opera and narrative song remain most people's musical life.
