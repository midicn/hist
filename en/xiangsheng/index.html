Xiangsheng

Xiangsheng is mostly talk, with set singing and impersonation, its tongue-twisters showing vocal control

Ban and Yan

When and where

Xiangsheng took shape in late Qing Beijing, cradled in the open-ground pitches of Tianqiao. Zhang Sanlu is said to have moved from octagonal-drum singing to comic talk, and Zhu Shaowen built a name writing in white sand and telling solo pieces; the art then spread to Tianjin, rooting in teahouses and variety halls. Beijing and Tianjin each produced masters and traded styles.

How it came about

Xiangsheng separated from the split singing of the full octagonal-drum show, first solo, then in pairs with one feeding and one joking, later in groups. It braids four skills, speech, imitation, comedy and song. Speech carries narration, imitation covers dialects and street cries, comedy hunts the laugh, and song means taiping lyrics plus learned impressions. Radio and television carried the routines from open ground into homes.

The sound's frame

Xiangsheng is verbal, its musical element concentrated in singing and mimicry. Taiping lyrics are its own song, beaten on bamboo clappers, paired phrases repeated, melody plain, the effect carried by diction and breath. Liuhuo impressions take on Peking opera, pingju, drum song and shidiao, aiming at a school's accent and dialect rather than pretty pitch. Tongue-twisters, gabble passages and dialect turns test the lips, fast without tangling.

What to listen for

Begin with Tianjin Lianhualuo, which shares the bamboo clappers with taiping lyrics; hear how lines close. Then Tianjin Manjiang hong, a shidiao tune, to compare with the exaggeration of comic mimicry. Wang Erjie sifu is a drum-song standard, its narrative lines broken into laughs. All three are folk songs, not xiangsheng recordings.

What is often mistaken

People say xiangsheng is all talk, or that its singing is casual humming. Its song has a specific sense, taiping lyrics being the household form, while impressions must resemble a particular school. It is not Tianjin's alone; Beijing's Tianqiao is the source, and the two cities have fed each other ever since.
