Music History

Music history, period by period — from early music to the present, with the key milestones, representative works and listening cues of each era.

Periods

  • Contemporary1945 to today: serialism, electronics, minimalism and spectral music run in parallel.
  • Modern1900–1945: tonality was taken apart, and rhythm and form were reinvented.
  • Impressionist1890–1920: harmony became colour, and music stopped rushing to resolve.
  • Romantic1820–1900: individuality, piano literature and the art song pushed music toward personal expression.
  • Classical MusicClear phrasing, sonata form and public concerts turned music into an art for the concert-going public.
  • Baroque MusicBasso continuo, major-minor tonality and dramatic expression gave birth to opera, the concerto and the fugue.
  • Renaissance MusicEqual voices, imitation and music printing carried music from church to court and town.
  • Medieval MusicFrom plainchant to polyphony, the church, notation and the first named composers shaped Western music's beginnings.

Milestones

  • The Rise of the ConductorFrom the 1820s a baton turned the orchestra from a time-keeping group into an interpreted body.
  • The Piano Recital Is BornIn 1840 Liszt filled a whole evening alone at the piano, and the recital took its name.
  • The Birth of Film MusicIn 1908 Saint-Saëns wrote an original score for a film, and music began to follow the image.
  • Musique Concrète, 1948Schaeffer turned trains and pans into material, making sound itself the stuff of composition.
  • AtonalityVienna, 1908: the home key disappears, and harmony stops coming home.
  • Basso ContinuoA bass line and a few figures let players realise the harmonic frame on the spot.
  • Bayreuth 1876Wagner's festival theatre hid the orchestra and darkened the hall, inventing the composer's festival.
  • Birth of OperaFlorence, 1600: for the first time, sung drama carries an entire story.
  • Birth of the String QuartetFour strings learn to converse as equals, and chamber music finds its central genre.
  • Birth of the SymphonyThe opera overture leaves the stage, and the symphony becomes an independent public genre.
  • Equal TemperamentTwelve equal semitones let every key work on one instrument and made free modulation normal.
  • Guido's HandHow staff lines and solmization syllables made pitch writable, teachable and portable.
  • Jazz Enters the Concert HallNew York, 1924: jazz rhythm takes a seat in the concert hall.
  • MIDI 1.0In 1983, synthesizers from different makers finally speak one language.
  • Mensural NotationNote shapes began to record duration, letting polyphony be designed with exact proportions.
  • MinimalismIn C, 1964: repetition and phase difference rebuild a tonal music.
  • MonodyOne voice and a chordal instrument put the text and its feelings back at the centre.
  • Piano Replaces HarpsichordHammers, pedals and dynamic marks teach keyboard music to breathe.
  • Printed MusicMovable type turned music into a commodity and let repertory travel faster than scribes.
  • Radio BroadcastingIn the 1920s, music enters the home live for the first time.
  • The Birth of the ConcertoSolo and tutti answer each other, and contrast becomes a Baroque instrumental genre.
  • The First Public Opera HouseVenice began selling opera tickets in 1637, turning court spectacle into a public business.
  • The Mature Sonata FormExposition, development and recapitulation let instrumental music stage conflict without words.
  • The Ninth PremiereVienna, 1824: the human voice enters the symphony's final movement.
  • The Paris ConservatoireParis, 1795: the state trains professional musicians with a fixed curriculum.
  • The Phonograph, 1877Sound was captured and replayed for the first time, creating records, labels and stars.
  • The Public ConcertTickets, rented halls and subscriptions turn music from court service into a market.
  • The Rite of Spring PremiereThe 1913 Paris premiere caused a riot and made rhythm and dissonance a modernist manifesto.
