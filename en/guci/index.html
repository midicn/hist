Guci, Drum-Song Storytelling

Guci is the northern drum-and-three-string narrative genre of the Ming and Qing, built on banqiang metre and the thirteen rhymes

2 min readChinese MusicUpdated 2026-10-30

Time and place

Guci arose in northern towns in the late Ming and early Qing, mainly in Shandong, Hebei and Beijing, and moved into teahouses and storytelling halls in the late Qing and Republican period. Jia Fuxi's Mupi Sanren Guci represents early literati participation. From the mid-Qing, branches such as Shandong Dagu, Xihe Dagu and Jingyun Dagu matured, the last associated above all with Liu Baoquan.

What happened

Guci divides a long story into episodes. The performer beats a drum and clapper, accompanied by sanxian or sihu, speaking one passage and singing the next. Lyrics run mainly in seven- or ten-character lines, rhymed by the thirteen categories, and can stretch over many sessions. In the late Qing and early Republic, performers entered urban halls, worked with writers on texts, and developed set aria patterns from local tunes.

Musical features

Guci belongs to banqiang metre: a pair of upper and lower phrases generates the whole piece through changes of tempo and pattern, such as slow board, fast board and piled phrases. The drum and clapper mark openings and sections, the sanxian follows the voice, and the sihu thickens it. Singing is syllable-dense and stresses clear articulation and rhyme, with mood controlled by speed, register and drum patterns rather than complex modulation.

What to listen for

Hear Daoqing Shi Shou, where the fish-drum and clapper mark sections and the voice shifts between verse and prose. Then hear Lianhualuo, with its lead-and-response and crisp line endings. Finally hear Cuilian Juan, a long verse narrative pushing the story forward episode by episode. None is guci; they show the narrative pace and rhyme of northern storytelling.

Common misunderstandings

The common assumption is that all guci uses a drum. Daoqing uses a fish-drum, Lianhualuo uses bamboo clappers, and guci is only one branch. Jingyun Dagu is not Peking opera either; it is storytelling, with no acted roles. Nor is guci specific to Shandong: Hebei, Beijing and the northeast all have branches with different tunes and repertories.
