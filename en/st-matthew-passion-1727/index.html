The Saint Matthew Passion Premiere

Leipzig, 1727, where Bach set the Passion for double choir and made Good Friday worship a musical drama.

1 min readMilestonesUpdated 2026-10-28

BachPassionLeipzig1727

Related

BaroqueCantataAriaOrgan

The year it happened

Good Friday, 11 April 1727, in Leipzig's Thomaskirche. Bach directed the Passion as Thomaskantor, with two choirs facing each other and two orchestras plus organ on either side.

The sequence of events

Picander's libretto alternates Gospel narration, chorales and free arias. A tenor sang the Evangelist, a bass sang Christ, and the crowd's shouts went to the choir. Bach directed from within the church; the music had to fit inside a service of several hours, not a concert. He revised the work for later performances, and the versions of 1729 and 1736 differ in scoring and layout.

What it rewrote

The Passion became a large musical drama of narration, lament and congregational memory. Chorales gave listeners a voice at crucial moments; arias made them pause before one person's grief. The work was not printed in Bach's lifetime. Mendelssohn's revival in Berlin in 1829 brought it back into public memory and gave the Bach revival its strongest evidence.

Hear the turn

Begin with No. 39, Erbarme dich, where solo violin and alto stretch a single plea for mercy. Then the Passion chorale, a tune the congregation knew, polished and darkened by Bach's harmony. Finally a piano version of the Coffee Cantata, same city, same composer, speaking in a secular voice in a coffee house. Three settings, three ways of listening.
