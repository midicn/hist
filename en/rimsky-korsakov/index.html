Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov

A naval officer turned master orchestrator, whose Scheherazade teaches listeners to hear colour

Related

RomanticProgram MusicSuiteViolin

Who he was

Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov (1844–1908) was born in Tikhvin into a noble family. He entered the naval school as a boy, met Balakirev in 1861 and began composing seriously; in 1862 he went to sea, and after returning he wrote music while still in service. In 1871 he was invited to teach at the St Petersburg Conservatory, later inspected naval bands, and died in 1908.

His place

Rimsky-Korsakov belonged to the Mighty Handful and was a central figure of the Russian national school. He was drawn to oriental subjects, fairy tales and folk legend, setting Russian melody and exotic scales inside a brilliantly coloured orchestra. Unlike Mussorgsky's roughness, he prized craft and clear orchestration, and in later years he edited and completed his friends' unfinished works.

What to hear

Listen first for the colour of a solo: Scheherazade gives the storytelling to a solo violin, and the same melody returns each time on a different instrument. Then exotic scales: augmented seconds and pentatonic figures take the music away from German functional harmony, sounding like another place. Finally orchestral layers: woodwind, brass and strings each keep their own register, and the stack stays clear, which is his most admired craft.

Key works

  • Scheherazade, symphonic suite
  • Capriccio espagnol, Russian Easter Festival Overture
  • Operas Sadko and The Golden Cockerel, Principles of Orchestration

Influence

Rimsky-Korsakov's orchestration manual shaped generations of composers, and both Stravinsky and Prokofiev studied with him. He made timbre itself a structure, not merely clothing for a melody.
