Ravel

The orchestrator as watchmaker — the most Classical craftsman among the Impressionists

A life in brief

Maurice Ravel (1875–1937) was born in the French Basque country and lived most of his life in Paris. His output is small, yet nearly every work is core repertoire: the piano pieces Gaspard de la nuit and Miroirs, the orchestral Daphnis et Chloé and Boléro, and the opera L'Heure espagnole.

The city he worked in

Ravel is often grouped with Debussy as an "Impressionist", but he is more a Classical craftsman: clear structure, exact proportion, orchestration calculated like a watch. He absorbed Spanish folk music, jazz and non-Western scales, but always organised them with strict form.

What to hear

Listen first for layers of orchestration: every instrument has a defined job, and timbre is arranged like a palette. Then hear the precision of repetition: Boléro repeats one rhythm and one melody to the end, thickening through orchestration — repetition itself is the structure. Finally hear cool emotion: Ravel rarely vents directly; feeling hides in colour and proportion.

Key works

  • Piano: Gaspard de la nuit, Miroirs, Le Tombeau de Couperin
  • Orchestral: Daphnis et Chloé, Boléro, Rapsodie espagnole
  • Chamber: String Quartet, Piano Trio
  • Opera: L'Heure espagnole, L'Enfant et les sortilèges

What remains

After Ravel, orchestration is not just "adding colour" but a precise compositional technique; his harmony and rhythm shaped jazz, film scoring and contemporary orchestral writing. He proved that precision can be spellbinding.
