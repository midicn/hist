The Birth of Film Music

In 1908 Saint-Saëns wrote an original score for a film, and music began to follow the image.

1 min readMilestonesUpdated 2026-10-28

music historyfilm musicSaint-Saënssilent film

Year and coordinates

Paris, 28 December 1895: the Lumière brothers' first paid projection at the Grand Café. Paris, November 1908: Saint-Saëns wrote his score for The Assassination of the Duke of Guise.

The sequence of events

Early screenings used a piano or violin to cover the projector's noise, while players improvised watching the image. In 1908 the studio Le Film d'Art asked Saint-Saëns for a complete score for a serious drama. Written scene by scene and performed with the picture, it was the first original film score by a famous composer. Cue sheets and music libraries followed, letting cinemas choose pieces by mood. In 1927 The Jazz Singer brought sound film, and music moved from live accompaniment onto a fixed sound track.

What was different after

Music became part of narrative, telling audiences in advance whether to fear or laugh. Composers entered the film industry; scoring became work that could be produced, copied and licensed, and synchronization rights grew around it. Recorded sound replaced improvising players and cinema orchestras dissolved, but film music became a distinct craft.

Hear the turn

The library has no score for The Assassination of the Duke of Guise. Begin with Saint-Saëns's Danse macabre, whose orchestra paints a scene to the bone; then Joplin's Maple Leaf Rag and The Entertainer, popular pieces a silent-film pianist kept at hand, syncopation beating time for the image.
