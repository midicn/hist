G-Funk

Los Angeles slow rap, with deep bass, a thin gliding synth lead and P-Funk samples

G-funk is early-1990s Los Angeles rap, laying street narratives in sunlight with slow bass, a thin gliding synth lead and P-Funk samples.

Origins and era

In the late 1980s Los Angeles producers took material from Parliament-Funkadelic, Zapp and 1970s soul, putting synth glides and long bass notes into rap backing. Dr. Dre's The Chronic in 1992 layered Moog bass, a thin gliding lead and P-Funk samples in the studio, and its release on Death Row made it the West Coast template; Warren G with Nate Dogg pushed it onto radio in 1994. The name is usually read as gangsta joined to funk, and is often linked to George Clinton's P-Funk. Above the Law and DJ Quik offered harder and lighter versions at the same time.

What you hear

Tempos mostly run from 90 to 95 BPM. Bass is deep with long notes, and the synth plays a thin lead with portamento, closer to a whistle than a piano. Moog or other synths lay the bass, notes are joined by slides, and the drums are often sampled from 1970s funk records. Chords are soft and reverb is wide. Voices are relaxed and phrases stretch, female lines answer in the chorus, and the drums leave plenty of gaps. The overall feel is slower and warmer than the East Coast, with more room around everything.

Key figures

Dr. Dre's production and mixing defined the timbre, Warren G wrote sweeter melodies, Nate Dogg's voice became a signature, and Snoop Dogg's delivery sat inside the drums; Above the Law's Cold 187um and DJ Quik offered other production paths, while old records by George Clinton and Zapp supplied the raw material.

Lineage

P-Funk, Zapp's talk box, 1970s soul and West Coast rap feed in; West Coast rap, neo-soul's sense of bass and contemporary Los Angeles rap come out, and in the 2010s a new generation sampled the glide and bass back into their own records. It repackaged funk bass and glide at rap tempo.

Common misunderstandings

G-funk is not all West Coast rap, since Los Angeles produced plenty of records that did not work this way. Nor is it only street lyrics, because timbre and tempo define it too. The thin lead is usually a synth with portamento rather than a live instrument, and the talk box is a related but separate path.
