Nuo Opera

Nuo opera grew from exorcist ritual, masked, marked by gongs and drums, with one voice answered by many

1 min read0 characters条目Chinese MusicUpdated 2026-10-30

Time and place

Nuo opera is scattered across Wu'an in Hebei, southern Shaanxi, Guichi in Anhui, Nanfeng in Jiangxi, western Hunan and Dejiang in Guizhou, performed mostly at New Year, temple fairs and vow fulfilments. It grew out of ancient exorcist ritual and by the Ming and Qing had the scale of theatre, under names such as nuotang xi, duangong xi and tiaonuo.

What happened

Exorcism was a year-end rite to drive out pestilence, the exorcist, masks and drumming moving from house to house. Stories and characters entered, gods and demons acquired names and dialogue, and sung sections settled into fixed form, becoming plays. Performance stayed tied to ritual, summoning gods at the opening and sending them off at the close, actors masked, roles mostly mountain-openers, judges, earth gods and monks. The team plays of Guyi in Hebei, the duangong plays of Hanzhong and the nuotang plays of Guizhou each went their own way.

Musical features

Singing is plain, mostly paired phrases repeated, narrow in range, close to local folk song and storytelling. Percussion is the frame; drum, large and small gongs and cymbals mark the start and end of sections, with no strings to support the voice. One leads and the group answers, filler syllables abound, phrase ends often closed in unison. Words are mostly seven-character lines with spoken passages in dialect. Masks and steps carry the role's weight; the music only drives the scene.

What to listen for

Begin with Hebei Tiaoshen, ritual song and dance, hearing how the drum drives the steps. Then Xiashen diao, short repeated phrases, noting where lead and chorus meet. Duangong diao comes from Shaanxi duangong theatre, where dialect tones decide the melodic height. All three are folk songs, not complete nuo performances.

Common misunderstandings

Nuo is often equated with Guizhou nuotang opera alone, or dismissed as superstition. It exists in many provinces with distinct tunes and plays; it is a performance where ritual and theatre overlap, with established troupes, masks and transmission, not random spirit dancing.
