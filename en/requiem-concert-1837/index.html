The Requiem Enters the Concert Hall

Paris in 1837, when the requiem becomes a public musical event beyond the liturgy.

1 min read25 characters条目MilestonesUpdated 2026-10-28

requiemBerliozVerdiBrahms

Related

The year, the place

Paris, December 1837, at Les Invalides. Berlioz composed the Grande Messe des morts to honour the dead, with orchestra and four brass groups at the corners of the building. In May 1874 Verdi's Requiem was premiered at San Marco in Milan, then moved into theatres and halls. Brahms's Ein deutsches Requiem followed in Bremen in 1868.

First, the event

A requiem had been a funeral Mass, its texts bound to ritual. Berlioz pushed space, scale and drama, so that the Dies irae brass seemed to sound from every direction. Verdi treated soloists, chorus and orchestra with operatic force, making the Dies irae a contest of fear and prayer. Brahms chose German biblical passages instead of the Latin text and wrote a work of consolation for the living.

Effects and aftermath

The requiem no longer belonged to the church alone. Composers could write it for public commemoration, state ceremony or the concert hall, with performances dependent on ticket sales, choral societies and halls. Soloists, conductors and recordings became central, and the requiem entered the standard choral repertory. The line between sacred and secular was redrawn; Britten and Henze extended the genre.

Hear the turn

In Berlioz's Dies irae listen to the distant brass, then to how space pulls the chorus apart. Verdi's Dies irae begins with a shudder in the low strings; when the chorus enters it sounds like a crowd's fear. Brahms opens with a slow procession and gentle harmony, making death something to console rather than judge.
