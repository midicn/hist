Dionisio Aguado

A Madrid guitar teacher who wrote methods, redesigned the instrument and, with Sor, founded modern guitar teaching

Related

ClassicalClassical GuitarEtude

Who he was

Dionisio Aguado (1784–1849) was born in Madrid and studied guitar early with Miguel García. In 1825 he went to Paris, lived there for more than a decade, became a friend of Sor, returned to Madrid around 1837, and died there in 1849. He wrote two methods and designed a guitar stand that held the body more firmly and produced a fuller sound. His works are mostly studies and short pieces, almost all serving teaching; he rarely wrote large concertos or opera fantasies.

His place

He belongs to the late Classical Spanish guitar school and, with Sor, helped turn guitar teaching into a system. The Escuela de guitarra of 1825 and the Nuevo método of 1843 set out his fingering and notation at different stages. He cared not only how to play but about the instrument itself, discussing the stand, string length and right-hand technique. Few guitarists of his time thought about playing, teaching and equipment together in this way. His methods contain long explanations of hand shape and tone that still read concretely today.

What to hear

Listen first for bass and melody: in Les Favorites the accompaniment is broken chords and the tune rides above, so hear whether the two keep separate paths. It can be set beside Sor's short pieces to compare how the two men treat accompaniment. Then for the shape of a short piece: the Eight Little Pieces are brief but still have an opening, a middle and a close. The Six Little Pieces can be heard as a companion set, comparing how the same composer writes at different lengths. Next, the repeated figure: the A minor study supports its melody with a steady accompaniment, and the left hand must stay firm. Finally, phrasing: the Allegro has clear cadences, so hear how each paragraph is closed.

Key works

  • Escuela de guitarra (1825), Nuevo método (1843)
  • Les Favorites, Eight Little Pieces, Six Little Pieces
  • Study in A minor, Allegro

Influence

Aguado and Sor together defined nineteenth-century guitar teaching; his methods were translated and adapted and shaped classrooms in Spain and France. Both of his tutors were reprinted several times during the century. He turned guitar teaching from experience into method, and made the instrument itself something that could be improved.
